\newslide{Lecture 4's Fork (Reminder)}

\slides{The demon as bookkeeping, not a resolved paradox.}

\slidesincremental{
* Cold bulk / hot bulk / kinetic layer at the gate (optional: the gate rule)
* Number flux can balance while energy flux does not
* Maxwellians all the way to $v_\star$ are an \emph{illegal} coarse graining
}

\notes{Re-show the compartment idea in one slide. Left and right bulks may be locally Maxwellian a mean free path from the membrane; the kinetic layer is not. The taught fork was that particle-flux stationarity and energy-flux stationarity cannot both hold if every velocity at the gate is drawn from a bulk Maxwellian. That is the course's construction; Szilard, Landauer and Ellis were parked. Worksheet 3 asked which description is legal for each compartment, and where the missing entropy is allowed to sit.}

\speakernotes{Do not re-run the flux-balance calculation. One sentence on the two ledgers, then move to the four families.}

\newslide{Four Families (Named in the Brief)}

\slides{Different books, not competing slogans.}

\slidesincremental{
* Illegal Maxwellian-to-gate (both ledgers cannot close)
* Physical trapdoor (Smoluchowski: door in the same bath)
* Memoryful policy (record fills; erasure on the ledger)
* One ensemble for the whole box (sorting as a fluctuation)
}

\notes{These four were the legal menu in the worksheet brief. They locate the cost in different places: a failed description, a thermalised door, a stored outcome that must later be erased, or Maxwell's own statistical second law. Models often collapsed all four into one fluent Landauer story. The review question is which family the dialogue was actually in.}

\speakernotes{Name the four; do not teach Smoluchowski or Landauer from scratch. The point is that they are different books.}

\newslide{What the Dialogues Actually Hit}

\slides{Twelve scripts. The course picture was present; so was the parked material.}

\slidesincremental{
* Nearly all: compartments / kinetic layer; second law; include the demon's DOF
* Most: crossings $\neq$ energy; membrane as velocity gate; where entropy sits
* Most also: Landauer / memory --- even though lecture 4 parked it
}

\notes{Tagging the LLM answers only: demon-versus-second-law and Maxwellian-compartments-plus-kinetic-layer appeared in almost every file; Landauer or memory erasure in about ten of twelve; number-versus-energy and Smoluchowski's trapdoor in most; ``where does the entropy sit'' in about half to two thirds. Liouville / phase space, detailed balance as a slogan, Feynman's ratchet, Szilard's engine and Sagawa--Ueda were rarer --- present when a model or a student chased them, not the taught core. The class therefore saw both the lecture-4 fork and a strong Grasshopper pull toward information-erasure language.}

\speakernotes{Keep this as cohort patterns. Praise dialogues that stayed with compartments and ledgers before accepting a Landauer close.}

\newslide{Where Entropy May Sit}

\slides{Curiosity question from the brief --- still open after ten turns.}

\slidesincremental{
* Gas entropy can fall under the sorting rule
* Cost may sit at the gate, the trapdoor, the record, or mutual information
* ``Information is physical'' is not yet a location
}

\notes{The mandated opening asked where the missing entropy is allowed to sit once the demon is drawn as compartments. Strong dialogues kept that question alive: which $H(\pi_i)$ you may write for each compartment, and whether both ledgers close. Weak closes treated Landauer's bound, or ``include the demon,'' as a location without saying which degree of freedom is the memory, or what happens if there is no record. The course answer at this stage is still the fork plus the four families --- not a derivation of $k_B T\ln 2$.}

\speakernotes{One beat: slogan versus location. Then the habit slide.}

\newslide{Grasshopper Default}

\slides{Models collapsed the fork into a single erasure story.}

\slidesincremental{
* Landauer / ``information is physical'' without a named record
* Trapdoor and memory treated as the same mechanism
* Rare oversell: Liouville, Szilard, Sagawa--Ueda as the taught core
}

\notes{About ten of twelve LLM threads invoked Landauer or erasure cost. That is not wrong as *one* family --- it is wrong as a fluent substitute for compartments and two ledgers, especially for an autonomous membrane with no record. Smoluchowski's trapdoor is a different book (door in the bath, no notebook). Good Skepticus asked which compartment the slogan was describing, whether both ledgers closed under Maxwellians-to-gate, and what happens if there is no memory to erase.}

\speakernotes{Name the trap without shaming. Bridge to habits: curiosity then press.}

\newslide{Good Skepticus on Maxwell}

\slides{What worked in the stronger dialogues.}

\slidesincremental{
* Curiosity first: where may the entropy sit?
* Then press: legal coarse graining per compartment; both ledgers?
* Refuse slogan closes; keep families distinct
}

\notes{Patterns worth keeping into Worksheet 4: open with a genuine location question; let the model commit; then chase consequences (no record; door in the bath; Maxwellians only in the bulks). Dialogues that stayed curious past turn two without accepting ``information is physical'' as finished did the job the brief asked for. Worksheet 4 is already out --- due 1 December, before Quiz 4 --- and Exchange 1 was meant to start before today's Good Regulator block.}

\speakernotes{Close the Maxwell review. One line on W4 due date, then Good Regulator at 10:30.}
