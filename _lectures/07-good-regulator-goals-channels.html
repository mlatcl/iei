The expensive items were the missing-measure multi-select, MaxEnt recovering Maxwell–Boltzmann from one mean constraint, and the exact sets on the die identity and the demon membrane picture.

The common incomplete selection was to keep the KL reading of relative entropy and drop the steam point — or to add the claim that a density erases the remaining measure choice. Week 4’s undeclared \(m\) was already a KL; declaring it does not invent a new object. Anthropomorphic entropy of steam is already a coarse-graining. Passing from bin counts to a density does not cancel that choice; shifting bins while holding the discrete probabilities fixed does not change discrete \(H\) either.

Several scripts kept the MaxEnt slogan and missed that a lone \(\langle E\rangle\) constraint already recovers the Boltzmann weights, or added a free claim about non-equilibrium stationarity. The discrete derivation ends at \(p_i\propto\exp(-\sum_k\lambda_k f_k(x_i))\); each \(\lambda_k\) is tied to a constraint, and energy is only one allowed \(f_k\). Uniform stays optimal only when the constraints are trivial.

About half the papers missed the exact set on the die item. The recurring distractor was the identity with a minus in front of the residual term. Total entropy is the entropy of the group weights plus the average entropy inside the groups; there is leftover uncertainty after the first cut. Soft overlapping allocations can leave \(H(G\mid X)>0\); an exclusive partition does not overlap.

Liouville, Detailed Balance, and the Trapdoor

Teach-back from Worksheet 3. Several dialogues landed on Liouville or detailed balance; lecture 4 never named either formally. Today seat them under the course fork: Liouville is the fine-grained kinematic fact; detailed balance is the equilibrium rate consequence; the two-ledger membrane argument is the same impossibility written in compartments. Trapdoor in passing — why it is hard to coarse-grain. No Hamiltonian derivation; no Smoluchowski simulation; no Landauer proof.

Liouville is not ``a more sophisticated detailed balance.’’ Detailed balance is the equilibrium rate statement that follows from microscopic reversibility plus a stationary measure. Liouville is the kinematic fact that Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space measure (and therefore the fine-grained Shannon entropy of the phase-space density). The Week 4 fork — number flux can close while energy flux cannot if every velocity at the gate is drawn from a bulk Maxwellian — is the course-native form of the same objection at the coarse grain students already have.

Under deterministic Hamiltonian flow the phase-space density \(\rho_t\) is transported by a volume-preserving map (Liouville’s theorem). The fine-grained Shannon entropy \(-\int \rho\log\rho\) is therefore invariant. The Gibbs–Ehrenfest account of the second law is that the entropy that increases is a coarse-grained entropy: one that first lumps microstates into cells (or Shannon groups) and only then computes \(H\). A selective membrane that looked like a many-to-one ``sorting map’’ — collapsing distinct incoming velocities onto one sorted side without putting the missing volume somewhere else — would violate that conservation. Where the volume goes is the same question as where the missing entropy sits.

Detailed balance is stronger than no net particle current.'' It requires that every microscopic transition be matched by its reverse at equilibrium. A velocity-selective gate that admits hot left$\to$right and cold right$\to$left, with no reverse partners of those same transitions, is exactly the kind of rule that breaks detailed balance. If the whole box is supposed to sit at one equilibrium, that rule cannot be the complete dynamics: either reverse processes appear once the gate's physical state is included, or some resource (a record, a colder hinge, an external drive) is being consumed. The dialogues that presseddetailed balance’’ were pressing this point — often against fluent Landauer closes that never named a record.

If left and right bulks are locally Maxwellian and the gate draws every crossing from those bulks, particle-flux stationarity and energy-flux stationarity cannot both hold at a nontrivial \(v_\star\). That is the taught fork. It does not mention phase space, yet it is the same impossibility: a complete equilibrium description cannot sustain useful sorting without a place for the missing entropy (or the missing reverse flux). Time-reversal as a slogan (several dialogues) is right; Liouville and detailed balance are how that slogan seats in the coarse-graining language the module already uses.

Smoluchowski’s trapdoor (and Feynman’s ratchet) resolve the autonomous mechanical demon without Landauer: the door (or pawl) is a physical degree of freedom in the same thermal bath; fluctuation–dissipation and detailed balance kill net sorting. Landauer’s erasure cost is a different book — a memoryful controller with a record that must later be reset. Models in Worksheet 3 often collapsed the two. Keep them distinct: trapdoor fails because the mechanism thermalises; memoryful demon fails (on the Landauer reading) when the record is erased into a bath.

Coarse-graining the trapdoor is awkward because the honest description does not lump the door into an existing bulk Maxwellian; it adds degrees of freedom (angle, angular momentum, coupling to the gas) and then applies detailed balance in the enlarged space. Treating the door as a frictionless open/closed decision without those thermal DOF is an illegal coarse grain — the same modelling sin as drawing every gate velocity from a bulk Maxwellian. Stories that keep the hinge ``cold’’ or insulated are smuggling a second bath or a prepared resource; once that is named, one has left the one-bath trapdoor family. Mutual information between door and gas can be a useful intermediate language, but it is not yet a Shannon group \(H(\pi_i)\) on the course’s compartment diagram.

About ten of twelve Worksheet 3 threads invoked Landauer or erasure. That is legitimate as one of the four families in the brief; it is illegitimate as a fluent substitute for compartments and two ledgers, especially for an autonomous membrane with no record. Bennett’s non-circular reading derives the erasure cost from logical irreversibility (phase-space compression) — again Liouville’s neighbourhood — but that derivation is not today’s board. Today’s fence: name the record, or leave this book.

These four were the legal menu in the worksheet brief. Liouville and detailed balance underwrite the first two at different grains; Landauer underwrites the third; Maxwell’s statistical second law underwrites the fourth. Models often collapsed all four into one fluent erasure story. The review question remains which family a claim is actually in.

About ten of twelve LLM threads invoked Landauer or erasure. Liouville and detailed balance appeared when a model or student chased them — now taught briefly so they are no longer ``out of syllabus’’ slogans. Good Skepticus asked which compartment the slogan described, whether both ledgers closed, and what happens if there is no memory to erase.

Patterns worth keeping into Worksheet 4: open with a genuine location question; let the model commit; then chase consequences (no record; door in the bath; Maxwellians only in the bulks). Good Regulator Overview

[edit]

Overview only. Full Conant–Ashby development — concavity argument, existence of a deterministic optimum, and the two caveats in depth — is the Week 8 review. Today’s job is the setup, the notation, and the slogan and its two caveats. Diagrams follow Joery’s oneshot figure in the good-regulator notes: disturbance \(D\), system state \(S\), action \(A\), reguland \(Z\).

Conant and Ashby (Conant and Ashby, 1970) often label the regulator \(R\). We write \(A\) for the action so the policy \(\pi(a\mid s)\) is first-class. The reguland — the variable whose entropy successful regulation minimises — is \(Z\). The system state is \(S\); the disturbance is \(D\). The cumulant generating function earlier today was \(\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\log Z(-\boldsymbol{\theta})\); that \(Z\) was the partition function, not the reguland — different objects, same letter in the physics tradition.

Figure: Cause-controlled regulation (left) acts on the disturbance \(D\); error-controlled regulation (right) acts on the state \(S\) that \(D\) caused.

A disturbance \(D\) causes events in a system \(S\); the regulator counters with an action \(A\); the pair produces a reguland \(Z=\phi(S,A)\) (Conant and Ashby, 1970). Cause-controlled regulation acts on \(D\) (or on an inferred model of \(D\)). Error-controlled regulation acts on the state \(S\) caused by \(D\), so by the data-processing inequality \(I(A;D)\le I(S;D)\). Both diagrams are shown above; the existence proof that an \(H(Z)\)-optimal policy may be taken deterministic waits for Week 8.

Successful regulation, for Conant and Ashby, minimises the entropy of the reguland: \(\min_\pi H(Z)\). Among optima there exists a deterministic policy \(A=h(S)\), equivalently \(H(A\mid S)=0\). That is the sense in which the regulator is a model'' --- deliberately weak. Do not collapse the slogan into\(A\) contains a rich model of \(S\)’’: a constant action also has \(H(A\mid S)=0\). Do not confuse low \(H(Z)\) with a desirable outcome. Requisite variety in Shannon form will use the same letters: residual uncertainty satisfies \(H(Z)\ge H(D)-I(D;A)\) when \(A\) is the regulatory response. Week 8 reviews the theorem properly and applies it to capability claims.

Ashby’s law of requisite variety in Shannon form (Ashby, 1956), written in today’s letters: residual uncertainty in the reguland is at least the disturbance entropy minus the information the action carries about the disturbance. Requisite variety asks how much relevant \(I\) is required; the Good Regulator Theorem asks how that \(I\) may be organised. Full development of both, with the concavity argument, is Week 8.

The Good Regulator Theorem, in entropy form, is an existence result: among policies that minimise \(H(Z)\) there is a deterministic one, \(A=h(S)\), equivalently \(H(A\mid S)=0\) (Conant and Ashby, 1970). The full concavity / extreme-point argument is in the notes include; today the diagrams and the slogan-with-fences are enough for assessment. Requisite variety remains the quantity bound \(H(Z)\ge H(D)-I(D;A)\), not the structure theorem.

Goals as Radon–Nikodym

Class test timer (Goal measure): 11:10

Conant and Ashby minimise the entropy of the reguland. In the reinforcement-learning reading developed with Joery, the long-run state–action occupancy is pushed through a fixed channel \(K(z\mid s,a)\) to an outcome law \(p_\pi\) on \(Z\). Minimising \(H(Z)\) is concave over the occupancy polytope, so a deterministic policy is optimal — the same structural reason as an ordinary MDP return, with a different objective.

When the ``good’’ outcomes are not the whole alphabet, introduce a goal measure \(\nu\) absolutely continuous with respect to a base \(\lambda\). Entropy relative to \(\nu\) differs from entropy relative to \(\lambda\) by the goal term \[ \mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\tfrac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}(Z)\bigr]=-\mathbb{E}[r(Z)],\qquad r=-\ln\tfrac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}. \] So ordinary reward maximisation is entropy minimisation under a tilted reference — the same change-of-measure grammar as Week 5’s KL / Radon–Nikodym block. Conant and Ashby’s good set \(G\) is the special case that puts smaller \(\nu\)-volume on \(G\) than on \(G^c\), so the goal term pays for landing in \(G\).

This is a course-level vocabulary map, not the Joery paper. Assessed: letters \(D,S,A,Z\); \(\min H(Z)\); \(H(A\mid S)=0\) with both caveats; \(r=-\ln(\mathrm{d}\nu/\mathrm{d}\lambda)\) as the goal/reward bridge to Week 5. Optional overflow: DPI Proof and Information Bottleneck

Data-Processing Inequality

[edit]

Mutual information is the pairwise case of multi-information: \(I(X;Y)=H(X)-H(X|Y)=H(Y)-H(Y|X)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)\). Week 2 defined it from the chain rule and stated the data-processing inequality. We prove it now, because this week’s object is \(I\).

The data-processing inequality (Cover and Thomas, 1991, Thm 2.8.1) is the no-go on \(I\). If \(X\to Y\to Z\) is Markov, then \(I(X;Z)\le I(X;Y)\). The first expansion of \(I(X;Y,Z)\) uses the chain rule and drops \(I(X;Z\mid Y)\) by the Markov property. The second expansion is at least \(I(X;Z)\) because conditional mutual information is non-negative. Equality holds when \(I(X;Y\mid Z)=0\) — \(Y\) is a sufficient statistic of \(X\) for \(Z\), or \(Z\) already captures everything \(Y\) knew about \(X\).

This is the same grammar as \(I+H=C\). Conservation says you cannot have both high stored correlation and high free uncertainty without bound. Data processing says you cannot increase stored correlation by further processing. A claim that an intelligent system will “just process more” and thereby recover information that a Markov stage has already destroyed is a DPI violation.

KL Decay and Grouping

[edit]

\[ \text{KL}\left( p\,\|\,\pi \right)=\text{KL}\left( w\,\|\,w_\pi \right)+\sum_i w_i\, \text{KL}\left( p_i\,\|\,\pi_i \right) \]

For a Markov process with stationary distribution \(\pi\), \(\text{KL}\left( p_t\,\|\,\pi \right)\) is nonincreasing — the data-processing inequality applied to the evolving law. (Physics literature often calls the same functional relative entropy.) Decompose \(p\) into group weights \(w\) and conditionals \(p_i\), and likewise \(\pi\) into \(w_\pi\) and \(\pi_i\). Then \[\begin{align} \text{KL}\left( p\,\|\,\pi \right)=\text{KL}\left( w\,\|\,w_\pi \right)+\sum_i w_i\, \text{KL}\left( p_i\,\|\,\pi_i \right). \end{align}\] The first term is how far the macro weights sit from equilibrium weights; the second is residual non-equilibrium inside groups. Local equilibrium is the regime \(\text{KL}\left( p_i\,\|\,\pi_i \right)\approx 0\) with \(\text{KL}\left( w\,\|\,w_\pi \right)\) still free to fall. Under deterministic Hamiltonian flow the fine-grained Shannon entropy of the phase-space density is conserved (Liouville); the Gibbs–Ehrenfest account of the second law is that only a entropy increases. Week 2’s die made the static split; this is the same algebra along a trajectory toward equilibrium.

Information engines and finite-time protocols act primarily on the between-group term — changing which macrostate or which bit is occupied — while assuming or enforcing fast decay of the within-group divergences. That is why week 2’s stationary examples and week 6’s paths on the equilibrium manifold belong to one story.

Information Bottleneck

[edit]

The data-processing inequality forbids creating information by processing. It does not say which information to keep. The information bottleneck (Tishby et al., 1999) is the prescription: given an observation \(X\) and a relevance variable \(Y\), choose a representation \(T\) of \(X\) that keeps \(I(T;Y)\) and discards the rest of \(I(X;T)\).

The information bottleneck of Tishby, Pereira and Bialek is a constrained optimisation on mutual information. The encoder \(p(t\mid x)\) is Markov given \(X\), so \(Y \leftrightarrow X \leftrightarrow T\). Data processing then supplies the bound \(I(T;Y)\le I(X;Y)\): no representation of \(X\) can be more informative about \(Y\) than \(X\) itself. The Lagrange multiplier \(\beta\) interpolates. Small \(\beta\) spends almost no \(I\) on the representation. Large \(\beta\) recovers \(T=X\). The self-consistent encoder is an exponential family, \[ p(t\mid x) \propto p(t)\,\exp\bigl(-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\bigl(p(y\mid x)\,\|\,p(y\mid t)\bigr)\bigr), \] the same Boltzmann form as week 4, now with a KL sufficient statistic. We do not run Blahut–Arimoto. The point is the pair: DPI is the no-go; the bottleneck is how you spend \(I\).

Week 4 asked for the least-committal \(p\) given moments. Here the constraint is a mutual information. Same Lagrange move, different constraint. Later in this session the bottleneck evaluates intelligence claims: a system that keeps only what is relevant for action is making an IB statement. The Good Regulator existence claim \(H(A\mid S)=0\) comes from concavity of entropy; only then may one offer the control interpretation \(\min I(S;A)\) subject to \(H(Z)\le\epsilon\) as an IB-shaped reading — an interpretation after the theorem, not the theorem itself. Requisite variety and the Good Regulator are tools we use in Week 8; the information bottleneck itself is the relevant-compression theorem.

IB as a gloss after GRT: \(\min I(S;A)\) subject to \(H(Z)\le\epsilon\) is a course interpretation, not Conant and Ashby’s equation.
