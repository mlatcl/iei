Subject: the Good Regulator and general intelligence

Modern talk of general intelligence often claims that a system which can model the world well enough can regulate it — plan, act, and succeed across domains. Conant and Ashby’s Good Regulator Theorem is frequently recruited into that story, sometimes under the slogan ``every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system.’’ Your job is to find out what an LLM says that slogan means, and whether the meaning it offers can support claims about AGI.

Models often collapse three different ideas: Ashby’s requisite variety (you need enough variety in the regulator), Conant–Ashby’s existence result about entropy-minimising policies, and contemporary claims about world-models and general agents. Use the dialogue to keep those apart.

Topic to Explore

Opening — Exchange 1 (curiosity). Ask what the Good Regulator Theorem is, and how it relates to modern notions of general intelligence. Let the model give a full answer. Do not steer it yet toward \(H(A\mid S)=0\), caveats, or the course notes. Invite an explanation that connects the theorem (as the model understands it) to AGI, foundation models, world-models, or ``systems that can regulate any environment.’’

Follow-up — Exchange 2 (skepticism). Press that answer. What exactly is being claimed: that a successful regulator contains a rich model of the system, or something weaker? If the model says must be a model,'' ask whatmodel’’ means operationally. If it links the theorem to AGI capability, ask which step turns an existence claim about policies into a guarantee of general intelligence. If it conflates the Good Regulator with requisite variety, ask it to separate them.

Later exchanges (3–10). Some ideas for follow-up once the opening pair is done. You need not use all of them; pick a thread and keep pressing.

  • Existence versus universality: the theorem says something about optimal regulators of a given regulation problem. Does that license claims about general intelligence across problems?
  • What is regulated? Conant–Ashby minimise \(H(Z)\). Is low entropy of the outcome the same as a desirable outcome?
  • Weak ``model’’: if \(A=h(S)\) is enough to count as a model, does a constant action \(A=a_0\) also count? When would optimality rule that out?
  • Requisite variety versus Good Regulator: Ashby demands enough variety (or enough relevant mutual information); Conant–Ashby organise an optimal policy. Which one are AGI slogans actually using?
  • Data-processing: can further processing of observations manufacture the missing information an agent needs? (DPI as a no-go; full proof comes in Week 7.)
  • World-models and foundation models: if a large model predicts tokens or pixels well, is that the sense of ``model’’ in the theorem — or a different claim altogether?
  • Course theme: for each claim the LLM makes, is entropy a no-go or a prescription?

Once you get the hang of it, take the conversation in your own directions. After lecture 6’s Good Regulator block (and Week 7’s DPI / information-bottleneck material), you may return for later turns or rewrite the submitted conversation — but keep Exchange 1 as a genuine pre-lecture curiosity probe in spirit, and do not stitch different chats.
