Role: You Are Skepticus

Large language models tend to give expansive answers to questions. This makes them very poor Socratic tutors; they are weak at sustained Socratic questioning.1 As in Worksheets 1 and 2, take the role of a skeptical questioner — Skepticus to the LLM’s Grasshopper.

  1. Curiosity then skepticism. An example opening pair is given below.
  2. Submit ten prompt/answer turns. Turns 1–2 follow the mandated topic; turns 3–10 are yours.
  3. Planning is open. You may use any materials or LLMs while planning. The submitted file must be the final exchange: ten turns from one continuous conversation with a single model. Do not stitch chats or switch models mid-file. Record that model’s name and version in the YAML header.

Subject: where can the entropy sit?

Lecture 4 left Maxwell’s demon as a fork, not a resolution. The two ledgers (particle flux and energy flux) do not close if every compartment is Maxwellian all the way to the gate. The next move is to coarse-grain Maxwell: which description you are allowed to write for each compartment, and what entropy each description may claim.

The lecture named four families. They are different books, not competing slogans:

  1. Illegal coarse graining. Maxwellians all the way to \(v_\star\). Particle-flux balance and energy-flux balance cannot hold together.
  2. Physical trapdoor. The door is a degree of freedom in the same bath; it thermalises (Smoluchowski 1914).
  3. Memoryful policy. The record fills; erasure is on the ledger (Szilard 1929; Landauer 1961).
  4. One ensemble for the whole box. Sorting is a fluctuation, not a cycle (Maxwell, Theory of Heat).

Your job is to test whether the model’s account of the demon is a legal coarse graining, an illegal Maxwellian-at-the-gate, or a slogan that never says where the entropy sits.

Models often collapse the demon into a single fluent story (usually Landauer, sometimes “information is physical”). Use the dialogue to expose which compartments that story is describing, and whether both ledgers close.

Topic to Explore

Opening. Ask where the missing entropy is allowed to sit once the demon is drawn as compartments. Invite a full explanation of why a selective membrane at \(v_\star\) looks like a second-law violation. Let the model commit to a location: the gate, the record, the trapdoor, the whole-box fluctuation, or somewhere else.

Follow ups. Some ideas for follow up. If the left and right bulks are locally Maxwellian, what is the kinetic layer at the membrane — a group of the same kind, or a different description? Can number current and energy current both vanish in a closed box if every velocity at the gate is drawn from a bulk Maxwellian? If the model invokes Landauer, ask which physical degree of freedom is the memory, and what happens if there is no record. If it invokes a trapdoor, ask whether the door is in the same bath. If it refuses compartments, ask what Maxwell meant by a being who handles individual molecules. For each family: what does entropy forbid, and what does the coarse graining prescribe?

  • Illegal Maxwellian-to-gate versus local equilibrium in the bulks only: which \(H(\pi_i)\) are you allowed to write?
  • Two ledgers: flux balance of \(N\) versus flux balance of \(U\). Detailed balance of velocities is a stronger claim; does the model notice?
  • Smoluchowski’s trapdoor / Feynman’s pawl: mechanism in the bath, not a notebook.
  • Szilard / Landauer: one bit of a sorting policy; erasure cost. Does that story apply to an autonomous membrane with no record?
  • Maxwell’s own statistical second law: sorting as a fluctuation of one ensemble.
  • Course theme: for each family, is entropy a no-go or a recipe?
