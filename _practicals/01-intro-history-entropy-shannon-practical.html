Role: You Are Skepticus

Large language models tend to give expansive answers to question. This makes them very poor Socratic tutors, they are very weak at sustained Socratic questioning.1 In response, we need you to take the role of a skeptical questioner, similar to Skepticus in Bernhard Suit’s Grasshopper.

  1. Curiosity then skepticism. An example is given with the suggested opening pair below.
  2. Submit ten prompt/answer turns. Turns 1-2 are suggested below, turns 3-10 are over to you.
  3. Planning is open. You can use any materials and any LLMs you like while planning your question sequence or testing a particular idea. But what you submit will be the final echange. That exchange will be ten turns from one continuous conversation with a single model. Do not stich turns from different chats. Do not switch model during the submitted chat. Your submission will have a YAML header. Use it to record the model’s name and version (or exact model id). You need not list or share practice runs or other tools. You will only submit the final conversation.

Subject: why did Shannon use the term entropy?

The subject of this dialogue is the history of the name. How did information theory come to borrow entropy from thermodynamics? What was the intellectual trail that word followed. Lecture 1 gave you Shannon’s bit as a unit and the course theme (entropy forbids; probability prescribes).

Of course there are different interpretations of what happened, the imposition of a single narrative is known as narrative fallacy. You will be using this session to expose the narrative fallacies.

Suggesgted opening query

Exchange 1 - curiosity. Ask why Shannon called his measure of information entropy. Let the model give a full answer. Many models default to a von Neumann cocktail-party anecdote. That is the raw material, not the end of the inquiry. You may have to vary your prompt to trigger the von Neumann anecdote.

Exchange 2 - skepticism. Press the answer against the primary source. In A Mathematical Theory of Communication (Shannon, 1948), when Shannon identifies \(H=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i\) with statistical-mechanics entropy, he footnotes Tolman, Principles of Statistical Mechanics. He cites von Neumann in passing and credits Wiener extensively in footnote 4. Challenge the model: if the naming story is von Neumann, why does the footnote credit Wiener so much? Where does Norbert Wiener fit?

You will need to do some work with the original sources to get the most interesting story out. See below for some suggestions.

Suggested directions for later exchanges

For additional context Wiener’s ideas about information grew from a “theory of ignorance” inspired by Gibbs. Had Wiener already talked about Gibbs entropy with Shannon? Why then is the cocktail-party story the default answer?

After the opening pair, choose a thread and keep pressing. Suggestions (pick one or mix).

  • What did Wiener think entropy was? Where did he get the idea??
  • How does Shannon’s move differ from Wiener’s. Think about continuous vs discrete distributions, stripping context, making information fungible?
  • Boltzmann’s \(H\)-theorem versus Gibbs’s ensemble entropy: which formulation is Shannon pointing at via Tolman?
  • Is calling \(H\) “entropy” a metaphor, an analogy, or an identity of mathematical form? What would have to be true for each reading?

Once you get the hang of it, take the conversation in your own directions.
