Exponential Family

Week 5’s discrete MaxEnt solution is an exponential family.

  • Sufficient statistics: \(T_k(x_i)=f_k(x_i)\), fixed by the constraints
  • MaxEnt: \(p_i=\exp\bigl(-\sum_k \lambda_k T_k(x_i)\bigr)/Z\)
  • Family: \(p(x\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})\propto \exp\bigl(\boldsymbol{\theta}\!\cdot\! T(x)-A(\boldsymbol{\theta})\bigr)\)
  • Identification: \(\boldsymbol{\theta}=-\boldsymbol{\lambda}\)
LO6. This is the block Week 5 only named. Physics sign: Boltzmann writes \(p_i\propto e^{-\beta E_i}\), so \(\theta_1=-\beta\) when \(T=E\). Fitting the \(\lambda\)s is maximum likelihood; say that and move to the three members. Geometry comes after the CGF slide, not here.

Canonical, Gaussian, and Bernoulli are the same theorem with different \(T(x)\).

  • Canonical: one energy constraint, \(T=E\), \(\theta=-\beta\)
  • Gaussian: mean and variance constraints
  • Bernoulli / two-level: \(T\in\{0,1\}\), \(\theta=-\beta\varepsilon\)
LO6 punchline. Two-level system is a Bernoulli. Softmax is the same calculation with a richer feature map; name it only if the clock is ahead. Do not start the matrix exponential family; that waits for the Week 8 quantum close if at all.

\(A(\theta)\) as Cumulant Generating Function

  • Week 3: \(F=-k_BT\log Z\) (available energy)
  • Same object: \(A(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\log Z(-\boldsymbol{\theta})\)
  • Mean parameters: \(\boldsymbol{\eta}=\nabla A=\langle T\rangle\)
  • Hessian: \(\nabla^2 A=\mathrm{Cov}[T]\)
Board \(\eta=\nabla A\) and \(\nabla^2 A=\mathrm{Cov}[T]\). The next slides read that Hessian as Fisher information. Do not write \(\mathcal{L}\) or \(\langle W_{\mathrm{ex}}\rangle\ge\mathcal{L}^2/\tau\).

Fisher Metric from the Exponential Family

Same word; different operational reading.

  • Shannon \(H\): uncertainty in an outcome
  • Fisher \(I(\theta)\): sensitivity of \(\log p\) to \(\theta\)
  • \(H\) is a state function of \(p\); \(I\) is a property of a family \(p(\cdot\mid\theta)\)
LO8. Hold this distinction for the rest of the module. Shannon is coming after the break as channel capacity; Fisher is already on the board as \(\nabla^2 A\).

On an exponential family the CGF Hessian is the Fisher information.

  • Score definition: \(G_{ij}=\mathbb{E}[\partial_i\log p\,\partial_j\log p]\)
  • Exponential family: \(G(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\nabla^2 A(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\mathrm{Cov}[T]\)
  • Same matrix: estimation, identifiability, geometry
This is why information geometry follows the exponential family. You already have \(G\); you are naming it. Cramér–Rao \(\mathrm{cov}(\hat\theta)\succeq G^{-1}\) in one sentence if asked; do not board a proof.

\[ \mathrm{d}s^2 = \mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta}^\top G(\boldsymbol{\theta})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta} \]

  • \(G\) is a Riemannian metric on the manifold of distributions
  • Large \(G\): nearby \(\theta\) are easy to tell apart from data
  • Small \(G\): the parameter is almost invisible in samples
State the line element. Do not integrate a path to get thermodynamic length \(\mathcal{L}\). Distinguishability of nearby points is enough; Crooks path cost is dropped.

Exponential families are dually flat under two coordinate systems.

  • Natural parameters \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\) (e-coordinates)
  • Mean parameters \(\boldsymbol{\eta}=\nabla A=\langle T\rangle\) (m-coordinates)
  • Hessians of the dual potentials are inverse metrics
  • Full Legendre / Pythagorean development stays notes
Name dual flatness; do not board \(H=A-\theta\cdot\eta\) unless ahead. The pair \((\theta,\eta)\) is enough to see why MaxEnt looks like an \(m\)-projection later. Natural gradient is optional colour only.

Shannon’s Communication Theorem

A channel is a conditional \(p(y\mid x)\). Rate cannot beat capacity.

  • Mutual information \(I(X;Y)\) is the channel currency
  • Capacity \(C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)\)
  • No-go: reliable communication requires \(R \le C\)
  • Prescription: the input \(p(x)\) that achieves \(C\)
Name Shannon’s noisy-channel coding / communication theorem. Do not prove the coding theorems. The BSC plot from the capacity include is enough geometry for today.

Scaffolding, Not Outcomes

Chain Rule, Mutual Information, Capacity

Four results we will need later — stated, not proved today.

  • Chain rule: \(H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y|X)\)
  • Mutual information: \(I(X;Y) = H(X)-H(X|Y) = H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)\)
  • Capacity: no rate above \(C\); achieving \(C\) requires the capacity-achieving \(p(x)\)
  • Data processing: if \(X\to Y\to Z\) is Markov, \(I(X;Z)\le I(X;Y)\)
Define \(I\) from the chain rule. State DPI; do not prove. Proof and the information bottleneck are week 8 (LO10). Capacity remains this week’s no-go/prescription pair.
Mutual information for a binary symmetric channel; capacity is achieved at uniform input.

Data-Processing Inequality

If \(X\to Y\to Z\) is Markov, processing cannot create information about \(X\).

  • Markov: \(p(z\mid x,y)=p(z\mid y)\)
  • Statement: \(I(X;Z)\le I(X;Y)\)
  • Soft partitions: leftover \(H(G\mid X)\) already spent information
  • Proof: Week 7 (Cover–Thomas 2.8.1)
State DPI; do not board the two expansions unless ahead of clock. Moral only: you cannot process your way to more \(I\). Week 7 proves it when multi-information is the object.
  • No-go: processing cannot create \(I\)
  • Week 7: prove DPI; information bottleneck as prescription

Good Regulator

Purely Entropic Good Regulator

Requisite Variety, Entropically

Residual outcome uncertainty is disturbance entropy minus information the regulator has about the disturbance.

  • Disturbance \(D\), response \(R\), essential variable \(E\)
  • \(H(E)\ge H(D)-I(D;R)\)
  • Useless variety appears as small \(I(D;R)\)
Named tool for LO13, not a new outcome. Ashby’s requisite variety in Shannon form (Ashby, 1956). Quantity, not structure. Perfect regulation \(H(E)=0\) forces \(I(D;R)=H(D)\).

Setup: Regulate by Minimising \(H(Z)\)

One shot, not an MDP: choose a policy \(\pi(a\mid s)\) to minimise outcome entropy.

  • State \(S\), action \(A\), outcome \(Z\sim p(z\mid s,a)\)
  • Objective: \(\min_\pi H(Z)\)
  • Model (weak): \(H(A\mid S)=0\), i.e. \(A=h(S)\)
LO13. Board this setup. Not Bellman, not reward. Entropy of \(Z\) is Conant and Ashby’s success criterion. Ten to twelve minutes for the proof block.

Concentration: Concavity of Entropy

Randomising between outcome-distinct actions cannot minimise \(H(Z)\).

  • At state \(s\), mix \(a_1,a_2\) with \(\psi(s,a_1)=z_1\ne z_2=\psi(s,a_2)\)
  • Transfer mass \(\alpha\): \((u,v)\mapsto(u-\alpha,v+\alpha)\)
  • \(g''(\alpha)<0\): interior mixture is not a minimum
Board \(g(\alpha)=-(u-\alpha)\log(u-\alpha)-(v+\alpha)\log(v+\alpha)\). Strict concavity is the engine. Deterministic \(\psi\) for this lemma.

Good Regulator Theorem

Among \(H(Z)\)-minimisers there exists a deterministic policy: \(H(A\mid S)=0\).

  • Pick one action per \(s\) among those sharing \(z^\ast(s)\)
  • \(A=h(S)\) leaves \(p(z)\), hence \(H(Z)\), unchanged
  • Stochastic channel: same existence via extreme points
Theorem: exists optimal \(\pi\) with \(H(A\mid S)=0\). Not every optimal policy is deterministic — only that a simplest one is. Course close remains: entropy forbids; probability prescribes.

Two Caveats

The theorem is weaker than the popular slogan.

  • Weak model: constant \(A=a_0\) also has \(H(A\mid S)=0\)
  • Low \(H(Z)\) means predictable, not desirable
  • Catastrophe with \(H(Z)=0\) is ``optimal’’ under entropy alone
Do not collapse \(H(A\mid S)=0\) into ``\(A\) contains a rich model of \(S\).’’ Faithful models need \(H(S\mid A)=0\) as well. Desirability needs a goal set \(G\), not entropy alone.

After the Theorem: IB Gloss

IB gloss, not the theorem: keep only distinctions that matter for action.

  • \(\min I(S;A)\) subject to \(H(Z)\le\epsilon\) — course reading
  • Deterministic \(A=h(S)\) gives \(I(S;A)=H(A)\)
  • Do not attribute that optimisation to Conant and Ashby
IB was LO10. This gloss sits after the earned theorem. Course punchline remains Week 1: entropy forbids; probability prescribes.
