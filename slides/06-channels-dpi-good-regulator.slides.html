Exponential Family

Exponential Family: \(\theta=-\lambda\)

Week 5’s discrete MaxEnt solution is an exponential family.

  • Sufficient statistics: \(T_k(x_i)=f_k(x_i)\), fixed by the constraints
  • MaxEnt: \(p_i=\exp\bigl(-\sum_k \lambda_k T_k(x_i)\bigr)/Z\)
  • Family: \(p(x\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})\propto \exp\bigl(\boldsymbol{\theta}\!\cdot\! T(x)-\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\bigr)\)
  • Identification: \(\boldsymbol{\theta}=-\boldsymbol{\lambda}\)
LO6. This is the block Week 5 only named. Physics sign: Boltzmann writes \(p_i\propto e^{-\beta E_i}\), so \(\theta_1=-\beta\) when \(T=E\). Fitting the \(\lambda\)s is maximum likelihood. Next slide: the base measure \(h_0\) is counting measure as a prior — then three members. Geometry comes after the CGF slide, not here.

Counting Measure Is a Prior

Weeks 4–5: every density is \({\mathrm{d}}P/{\mathrm{d}}\mu\). On a finite alphabet the unnoticed \(\mu\) is counting measure.

  • Counting \(\#\): each allowed label weight 1; \(\int f\,{\mathrm{d}}\#=\sum_x f(x)\)
  • Shannon: \(H(P)=\log N-\mathrm{KL}(P\|u)\) with \(u=\#/N\)
  • Exponential family: \(p=\exp(\theta\cdot T-\psi)\,h_0\), today \(h_0=\mathbf{1}[x\in\mathcal{X}]\)
  • Same role as Jaynes’ \(m\); not a new ``measure of uncertainty’’
Two minutes maximum. Counting measure is a prior: every label a priori as good as every other. The identity \(H=\log N-\mathrm{KL}(P\|u)\) is the discrete case of the relative-entropy story from Weeks 4–5. Do not open Lebesgue singularity or mixed measures live — those sit in the notes for the curious. Poisson \(1/k!\) and binomial \(\binom{n}{k}\) appear as \(h_0\) on the next distribution slides — name them there, do not derive. Move to the three members.

Three Members, One Family

Canonical, Gaussian, and Bernoulli are the same theorem with different \(T(x)\).

  • Canonical: one energy constraint, \(T=E\), \(\theta=-\beta\)
  • Gaussian: mean and variance constraints
  • Bernoulli / two-level: \(T\in\{0,1\}\), \(\theta=-\beta\varepsilon\)
LO6 punchline. Two-level system is a Bernoulli. Softmax is the same calculation with a richer feature map; name it only if the clock is ahead. The next block rewrites the Week 1 families: protect Bernoulli (\(h_0=1\), counting prior) then Poisson or binomial (\(h_0=1/k!\) or \(\binom{n}{k}\), discrete density of states). Gaussian names Lebesgue. Do not start the matrix exponential family; that waits for the Week 8 quantum close if at all.

Common Distributions as Exponential Families

Week 1 Families, Exponential-Family Form

Each named distribution from the probability review is an exponential family — and each carries a base measure \(h_0\).

  • Form: \(p=\exp\bigl(\boldsymbol{\theta}\!\cdot\! T-\psi\bigr)\,h_0\)
  • Two stories for \(h_0\): counting prior (\(h_0=1\)) versus combinatorial / continuum measure
  • Bernoulli, categorical: plain counting on the labels
  • Binomial, Poisson, multinomial: \(h_0=\binom{n}{k}\), \(1/k!\), \(n!/\prod x_j!\) — discrete density of states
  • Gaussian: Lebesgue (continuous counterpart of \(\#\))
Callback to the Week 1 named list. Protect two sentences: (1) Bernoulli \(h_0=1\) is counting measure as prior; (2) Poisson \(1/k!\) / binomial \(\binom{n}{k}\) show why the family carries an \(h_0\) at all. Live spine: Bernoulli then Poisson (or binomial) for the contrast; Gaussian for Lebesgue. Multinomial if clock allows.

Bernoulli and Binomial

Same \(\theta\); different \(h_0\). That is the measure story in one pair.

  • Bernoulli: \(\theta=\mathrm{logit}(\pi)\), \(\psi=\log(1+e^{\theta})\), \(h_0=1\) (counting)
  • Binomial: same \(\theta\); \(\psi=n\log(1+e^{\theta})\); \(h_0=\binom{n}{k}\) (multiplicity)

Bernoulli as Exponential Family

Bernoulli: Natural Parameter and \(\psi\)

\[ P(x\mid\theta)=\exp\bigl(x\,\theta-\psi(\theta)\bigr)\,h_0(x),\qquad x\in\{0,1\} \] * \(\theta=\log\dfrac{\pi}{1-\pi}\),\(T(x)=x\) * \(\psi(\theta)=\log(1+e^{\theta})\),\(h_0(x)=1\) (counting on \(\{0,1\}\))

Point one of two. Write \(h_0=1\) on the board: Bernoulli is MaxEnt relative to counting measure. Logit is \(\theta\); softplus is \(\psi\). Same object as the two-level system. Binomial / Poisson next flip \(h_0\) away from 1.

Binomial as Exponential Family

Binomial: Natural Parameter and \(\psi\)

\[ P(k\mid\theta)=\exp\bigl(k\,\theta-\psi(\theta)\bigr)\,h_0(k),\qquad k=0,\ldots,n \] * \(\theta=\log\dfrac{\pi}{1-\pi}\),\(T(k)=k\) * \(\psi(\theta)=n\log(1+e^{\theta})\),\(h_0(k)=\binom{n}{k}\)

Point two begins here. Same \(\theta\) as Bernoulli; \(\psi\) is \(n\) times the softplus. The new object is \(h_0=\binom{n}{k}\): without that multiplicity you are not looking at the binomial. Say ``discrete density of states’’ and move on — Poisson repeats the lesson with \(1/k!\).

Poisson and Multinomial

Poisson makes the non-constant \(h_0\) unavoidable: without \(1/k!\) you do not get Poisson.

  • Poisson: \(\theta=\log\lambda\), \(\psi=e^{\theta}\), \(h_0=1/k!\)
  • Categorical: \(h_0=1\) (counting again); multinomial: \(h_0=n!/\prod_j x_j!\)

Poisson as Exponential Family

Poisson: Why \(h_0\) Is Not Optional

\[ P(k\mid\theta)=\exp\bigl(k\,\theta-\psi(\theta)\bigr)\,h_0(k),\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}_0 \] * \(\theta=\log\lambda\),\(T(k)=k\),\(\psi(\theta)=e^{\theta}\) * \(h_0(k)=1/k!\) — without it, mean-count MaxEnt is geometric, not Poisson

Point two, sharpened. Board \(h_0=1/k!\) beside Bernoulli’s \(h_0=1\). One sentence: counting prior gave Bernoulli; combinatorial measure gives Poisson. Do not derive the geometric MaxEnt unless asked — naming the contrast is enough.

Multinomial as Exponential Family

Multinomial / Categorical: Natural Parameters and \(\psi\)

\[ P(\mathbf{x}\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})=\exp\bigl(\boldsymbol{\theta}\!\cdot\! T(\mathbf{x})-\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\bigr)\,h_0(\mathbf{x}) \] * \(\theta_j=\log\dfrac{\pi_j}{\pi_K}\),\(T=(x_1,\ldots,x_{K-1})\) * \(\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})=n\log\bigl(1+\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}e^{\theta_j}\bigr)\) * Categorical: \(n=1\), \(h_0=1\); multinomial: \(h_0=\dfrac{n!}{x_1!\cdots x_K!}\)

Same two-point moral: categorical \(h_0=1\) is counting on the labels; multinomial multiplicity is density of states again. Categorical is \(n=1\). Same log-sum-exp \(\psi\) as softmax. Do not open the unconstrained \(K\)-parameter chart live.

Gaussian

Continuous close: \(h_0\) is Lebesgue — the continuum counterpart of \(\#\).

  • \(T=(y,y^2)\); \(\theta_1=\mu/\sigma^2\), \(\theta_2=-1/(2\sigma^2)\)
  • \(h_0\): Lebesgue (not counting; not \(1/k!\))
  • \(\psi\) is quadratic in \(\theta_1\) and logarithmic in \(\theta_2\)

Gaussian as Exponential Family

Gaussian: Natural Parameters and \(\psi\)

\[ p(y\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})=\exp\bigl(\theta_1 y+\theta_2 y^2-\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\bigr)\,h_0(y) \] * \(\theta_1=\dfrac{\mu}{\sigma^2}\),\(\theta_2=-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}\),\(T(y)=(y,y^2)\) * \(h_0\): Lebesgue (continuum counterpart of counting \(\#\)) * \(\psi(\theta_1,\theta_2)=-\dfrac{\theta_1^2}{4\theta_2}-\dfrac12\log(-2\theta_2)-\dfrac12\log(2\pi)\)

Name Lebesgue as \(h_0\) so the discrete tour closes: \(\#\) / combinatorial \(h_0\) / \(\lambda\). Two numbers on the slide: precision-weighted mean and minus half precision. \(\psi\) is the ugly formula; board only \(\eta=\nabla\psi\) if asked.

\(\psi(\theta)\) as Cumulant Generating Function

\(\psi(\theta)=\log Z\) Generates Cumulants

  • Week 3: \(F=-k_BT\log Z\) (available energy)
  • Same object: \(\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\log Z(-\boldsymbol{\theta})\)
  • Mean parameters: \(\boldsymbol{\eta}=\nabla\psi=\langle T\rangle\)
  • Next: entropy from \(\log p\), then Fisher from the score
Board \(\eta=\nabla\psi\) first. Do not yet claim the Hessian is Fisher — that calculation is the next two slides. Do not write \(\mathcal{L}\) or \(\langle W_{\mathrm{ex}}\rangle\ge\mathcal{L}^2/\tau\).

Entropy of an Exponential Family

Entropy: the Lovely Form

\[ \log p(x\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})=\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T(x)-\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \]

  • Take \(-\mathbb{E}[\log p]\): \(H=\psi-\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\eta}\) when \(h_0=1\)
  • with \(\boldsymbol{\eta}=\nabla\psi=\langle T\rangle\)
  • In general: \(H=\psi-\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\eta}-\mathbb{E}[\log h_0(X)]\)
Board the two-line derivation for \(h_0=1\). \(\log p=\theta\cdot T-\psi\); expectation kills \(T\) to \(\eta\); \(H=\psi-\theta\cdot\eta\). After Poisson / binomial, state the general correction \(-\mathbb{E}[\log h_0]\) once so nobody applies the short form to a Poisson. Today the boarded algebra uses \(h_0=1\) on the allowed set.

Fisher from the Same \(\log p\)

Differentiate once; take a second moment.

  • Score: \(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\log p = T-\boldsymbol{\eta}\)
  • \(\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{score}]=0\); \(G=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{score}\,\mathrm{score}^\top]=\mathrm{Cov}[T]\)
  • But \(\boldsymbol{\eta}=\nabla\psi\), so \(G=\nabla^2\psi\)
LO8 punchline as a calculation, not a slogan. Score \(=T-\eta\) is immediate from \(\log p=\theta\cdot T-\psi\). Squaring and taking expectation is Fisher. Differentiating \(\eta=\nabla\psi\) again is the Hessian. Same matrix three ways: Cov, Fisher, \(\nabla^2\psi\).

Fisher Metric from the Exponential Family

Fisher’s Information \(\neq\) Shannon’s Entropy

Same word; different mathematical definition.

  • Shannon \(H\): uncertainty in an outcome — just earned as \(H=\psi-\theta\cdot\eta\) (when \(h_0=1\))
  • Fisher \(I(\theta)\): sensitivity of \(\log p\) to \(\theta\) — just earned as \(\mathrm{Cov}[T]=\nabla^2\psi\)
  • \(H\) is a state function of \(p\); \(I\) is a property of a family \(p(\cdot\mid\theta)\)
LO8 vocabulary slide after the calculation. They have already done the algebra; name the distinction. Flag the \(h_0=1\) caveat: after Poisson / binomial, the full form is \(H=\psi-\theta\cdot\eta-\mathbb{E}[\log h_0]\). Shannon after the break is channel capacity, a different use of the same \(H\).

Same Matrix, Three Names

You already have \(G\); you are naming it.

  • \(\mathrm{Cov}[T]\) from cumulants
  • Fisher \(G\) from the score \(T-\eta\)
  • \(\nabla^2\psi\) from differentiating \(\eta=\nabla\psi\)
  • Cramér–Rao: \(\mathrm{cov}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\succeq G^{-1}\) (name only)
One sentence on Cramér–Rao if asked; do not board a proof. Information geometry follows because the metric is already on the board.

Information Distance

\[ \mathrm{d}s^2 = \mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta}^\top G(\boldsymbol{\theta})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta} \]

  • \(G\) is a Riemannian metric on the manifold of distributions
  • Large \(G\): nearby \(\theta\) are easy to tell apart from data
  • Small \(G\): the parameter is almost invisible in samples
State the line element. Do not integrate a path to get thermodynamic length \(\mathcal{L}\). Distinguishability of nearby points is enough; Crooks path cost is dropped.

Dual Charts \((\theta,\eta)\)

Exponential families are dually flat under two coordinate systems.

  • Natural parameters \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\) (e-coordinates)
  • Mean parameters \(\boldsymbol{\eta}=\nabla \psi=\langle T\rangle\) (m-coordinates)
  • Hessians of the dual potentials are inverse metrics
  • Full Legendre / Pythagorean development stays notes
Name dual flatness. \(H=\psi-\theta\cdot\eta\) (when \(h_0=1\)) is already on the board — that is \(-\psi^*(\eta)\). The pair \((\theta,\eta)\) is enough to see why MaxEnt looks like an \(m\)-projection later. Natural gradient is optional colour only.

Shannon’s Communication Theorem

Shannon’s Noisy Channel Coding Theorem

A channel is a conditional \(p(y\mid x)\). Rate cannot beat capacity.

  • Mutual information \(I(X;Y)\) is the channel currency
  • Capacity \(C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)\)
  • No-go: reliable communication requires \(R \le C\)
  • Prescription: the input \(p(x)\) that achieves \(C\)
Name Shannon’s noisy-channel coding / communication theorem. Do not prove the coding theorems. The BSC plot from the capacity include is enough geometry for today.

Scaffolding, Not Outcomes

Chain Rule, Mutual Information, Capacity

Four results we will need later — stated, not proved today.

  • Chain rule: \(H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y|X)\)
  • Mutual information: \(I(X;Y) = H(X)-H(X|Y) = H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)\)
  • Capacity: no rate above \(C\); achieving \(C\) requires the capacity-achieving \(p(x)\)
  • Data processing: if \(X\to Y\to Z\) is Markov, \(I(X;Z)\le I(X;Y)\)
Define \(I\) from the chain rule. State DPI; do not prove. Proof and the information bottleneck are week 8 (LO10). Capacity remains this week’s no-go/prescription pair.
Mutual information for a binary symmetric channel; capacity is achieved at uniform input.

Data-Processing Inequality

Data-Processing Inequality

If \(X\to Y\to Z\) is Markov, processing cannot create information about \(X\).

  • Markov: \(p(z\mid x,y)=p(z\mid y)\)
  • Statement: \(I(X;Z)\le I(X;Y)\)
  • Soft partitions: leftover \(H(G\mid X)\) already spent information
  • Proof: Week 7 (Cover–Thomas 2.8.1)
State DPI; do not board the two expansions unless ahead of clock. Moral only: you cannot process your way to more \(I\). Week 7 proves it when multi-information is the object.
  • No-go: processing cannot create \(I\)
  • Week 7: prove DPI; information bottleneck as prescription

Good Regulator

Good Regulator Overview

Notation: \(D\), \(S\), \(A\), \(Z\)

The CGF was \(\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\). After the break, \(A\) is action.

  • \(D\): disturbance
  • \(S\): system state
  • \(A\): action (the regulator’s response; Conant–Ashby often write \(R\))
  • \(Z\): reguland / outcome (Ashby’s essential variable)
Board the four letters once. Classic cybernetics used \(R\) for the regulator; we use \(A\) for action so the policy language matches Weeks 7–8. The log-partition / CGF is \(\psi\), so there is no clash with action. Do not prove anything yet.

Two Setups

  • Cause-controlled: act on \(D\) (or on a model of \(D\))
  • Error-controlled: act on the state \(S\) that \(D\) caused
  • DPI: \(I(A;D)\le I(S;D)\) when \(A\) only sees \(S\)
Spend the clock on the diagrams. Cause control reads the disturbance before it enters the essential variable; error control corrects after the fact. Most modern RL policies are error-controlled on the observed state. Week 8 returns to why the simplest optimum factors as a model.

What ``Good’’ Means Today

Success is low entropy in the reguland — with fences.

  • Objective sketch: \(\min_{\pi(a\mid s)} H(Z)\)
  • Slogan: every good regulator must be a model of the system
  • Weak reading of ``model’’: \(H(A\mid S)=0\), i.e. \(A=h(S)\)
  • Full theorem and caveats: Week 8 review
Name the objective and the slogan. Do not board the concavity transfer \(g(\alpha)\). Two fences only: constant \(A=a_0\) also has \(H(A\mid S)=0\); low \(H(Z)\) is predictable, not desirable. Worksheet 4 Exchange 1 has already asked them what GRT has to do with general intelligence — leave the depth for Week 8.

Requisite Variety

Quantity bound, not the structure theorem.

  • \(H(Z)\ge H(D)-I(D;A)\)
  • Useless variety: large \(H(A)\) with small \(I(D;A)\)
  • Ashby: how much regulatory information is required
  • GRT (Week 8): how that information may be structured
One slide. Same \(A\) and \(Z\) as the diagrams. Perfect regulation \(H(Z)=0\) forces \(I(D;A)=H(D)\) under the usual bound.
