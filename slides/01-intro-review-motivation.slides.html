Preview: Landauer’s Principle

Erasing a bit has a thermodynamic price. Computation, in the reversible ideal, is claimed not to.

  • Erase one bit: at least \(k_B T\ln 2\) of heat (Landauer, 1961)
  • \(k_B \sim 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K}^{-1}\); at room temperature this is tiny
  • Classical computers dissipate far above the bound; we are not at Landauer yet
  • Why erasure answers the demon — memory, or an infinite tape — is Week 4
If asked how Landauer answers the demon: memory must grow or be erased; infinite tape will not fit in the box. Do not derive \(k_B T\ln 2\). Do not open Szilard.

Probability Review

Joint, Marginal, Conditional

  • Joint \(P(x,y)\): both
  • Marginal \(P(x)\): regardless of \(y\)
  • Conditional \(P(x\mid y)\): \(x\) given \(y\)

Product Rule and Sum Rule

  • Product: \(P(x,y)=P(x\mid y)P(y)\)
  • Sum: \(P(y)=\sum_x P(x,y)\)
  • Both are normalising bookkeeping, not modelling assumptions

Bayes’ Rule

\[ P(y\mid x)=\frac{P(x\mid y)P(y)}{P(x)} \]

  • Follows from the product rule and symmetry of the joint
  • Inverts the conditioning — updates a prior given a likelihood
  • \(P(x)=\sum_y P(x\mid y)P(y)\) when \(y\) is discrete

Bayes’ Theorem Example

  • There are two barrels in front of you. Barrel One contains 20 apples and 4 oranges. Barrel Two other contains 4 apples and 8 oranges. You choose a barrel randomly and select a fruit. It is an apple. What is the probability that the barrel was Barrel One?
Two barrels: Barrel One holds 20 apples and 4 oranges; Barrel Two holds 4 apples and 8 oranges. A barrel is chosen uniformly at random and a single fruit is drawn.

Bayes’ Rule Example: Answer I

  • We are given: \[\begin{aligned} P(\text{F}=\text{A}|\text{B}=1) = & 20/24 \\ P(\text{F}=\text{A}|\text{B}=2) = & 4/12 \\ P(\text{B}=1) = & 0.5 \\ P(\text{B}=2) = & 0.5 \end{aligned}\]

Bayes’ Rule Example: Answer II

  • We use the sum rule to compute: \[\begin{aligned} P(\text{F}=\text{A}) = & P(\text{F}=\text{A}|\text{B}=1)P(\text{B}=1) \\& + P(\text{F}=\text{A}|\text{B}=2)P(\text{B}=2) \\ = & 20/24\times 0.5 + 4/12 \times 0.5 = 7/12 \end{aligned}\]
  • And Bayes’ rule tells us that: \[\begin{aligned} P(\text{B}=1|\text{F}=\text{A}) = & \frac{P(\text{F} = \text{A}|\text{B}=1)P(\text{B}=1)}{P(\text{F}=\text{A})}\\ = & \frac{20/24 \times 0.5}{7/12} = 5/7 \end{aligned}\]

Common Distributions

Named Distributions You Need

Five families recur across modelling. Know the support, the parameter, and one generative story for each.

Bernoulli and Binomial

  • Bernoulli(\(p\)): single binary trial; \(P(X=1)=p\)
  • Binomial(\(n,p\)): \(n\) i.i.d. Bernoulli trials; count of successes
  • Mean \(np\), variance \(np(1-p)\)

Poisson and Multinomial

  • Poisson(\(\lambda\)): counts in a fixed interval; mean \(=\) variance \(=\lambda\)
  • Multinomial(\(n,\mathbf{p}\)): \(n\) trials into \(K\) categories; generalises the binomial
  • Categories are exclusive; \(\sum_k p_k = 1\)

Poisson Distribution

  • Poisson distribution is used for ‘count data’. For non-negative integers, \(y\), \[P(y) = \frac{\lambda^y}{y!}\exp(-\lambda)\]
  • Here \(\lambda\) is a rate parameter that can be thought of as the number of arrivals per unit time.
  • Poisson distributions can be used for disease count data. E.g. number of incidence of malaria in a district.

Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution.

Gaussian

  • \(\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)\): continuous density on \(\mathbb{R}\)
  • Fixed by mean and variance; MaxEnt under those constraints
  • Multivariate form: mean vector and covariance matrix

The Gaussian Density

  • Perhaps the most common probability density.

\[\begin{align} p(y| \mu, \sigma^2) & = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(y- \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\\& \buildrel\triangle\over = \mathscr{N}\left(y|\mu,\sigma^2\right) \end{align}\]

Gaussian Density

The Gaussian PDF with \({\mu}=1.7\) and variance \({\sigma}^2=0.0225\). Mean shown as cyan line. It could represent the heights of a population of students.

Gaussian Density

\[ \mathscr{N}\left(y|\mu,\sigma^2\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\left(-\frac{(y-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \]

\(\sigma^2\) is the variance of the density and \(\mu\) is the mean.

Energy in Machine Learning

Energy as a Score

In ML, an energy is (often) a scalar assigned to a configuration: lower means preferred.

  • Losses are energies over parameters or predictions
  • Energy-based model: \(p(x)\propto e^{-E(x)}\) — same grammar as Boltzmann
  • Minimising \(E\) is the zero-temperature / MAP limit

Energies from Distributions You Already Know

From \(p\) to \(E\): the Four Outcomes

Earlier we tabulated \(-\log P(y)\) for a four-outcome distribution. Those values are energies.

\(y\) 1 2 3 4
\(P(y)\) 0.3 0.2 0.1 0.4
\(E(y)=-\log P(y)\) 1.204 1.609 2.302 0.916
  • Most probable outcome \(y=4\) has the lowest energy
  • Rarest \(y=3\) has the highest energy
  • Shannon entropy was \(\mathbb{E}[E(y)] = 1.280\) nats — average energy under \(p\)
Point at the same table from the entropy expectation slide. Do not recompute; only rename \(-\log p\) as \(E\).

Bernoulli / Fair Coin

A coin with \(P(H)=p\), \(P(T)=1-p\) is a two-state energy model.

Bernoulli: \(P\) and \(E\)

Bernoulli(\(p=0.3\)). Left: the distribution. Right: energy \(E=-\log P\) in nats. The favoured outcome sits lower in energy.

Bernoulli: Overlay

Same Bernoulli: probability (bars, left axis) and energy (markers, right axis). Where \(P\) is high, \(E\) is low.

Bernoulli Energy

  • \(E_0=-\log(1-p)\),\(E_1=-\log p\)
  • Fair coin: degenerate energies \(E_0=E_1=\log 2\)
  • Biased coin: the favoured face is the deeper well

Poisson: Count Energy

A Poisson(\(\lambda\)) is the same map \(E=-\log P\) on the non-negative integers.

Poisson: \(P\) and \(E\)

Poisson(\(\lambda=4\)). Left: the count distribution. Right: energy \(E=-\log P\). The mode is the energy minimum.

Poisson: Overlay

Same Poisson: probability (bars) against energy (markers). Tails are high-energy, low-probability states.

Poisson Energy

  • \(E(k)=-\log P(k)=\lambda-k\log\lambda+\log(k!)\) (up to the usual reading of Stirling)
  • Mode of \(P\) \(=\) minimizer of \(E\)
  • Same \(p\propto e^{-E}\) grammar as the Bernoulli two-state model

Gaussian: Quadratic Energy

The univariate Gaussian we wrote earlier is already an energy-based model.

\[ p(y)\propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{(y-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E(y)=\frac{(y-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \]

Gaussian: \(p\) and \(E\)

Height Gaussian. Left: density. Right: energy \(E=-\log p\) — a parabola about \(\mu=1.7\) (additive constant from the normaliser does not change the shape).

Gaussian: Overlay

Same Gaussian: density (left axis) and energy (right axis). The mode of \(p\) is the bottom of the energy well.

Gaussian Energy

  • \(E(y)=\frac{(y-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\) up to an additive constant absorbed in \(Z\)
  • Least-squares regression uses the same quadratic energy on residuals
  • Normaliser \((2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\) is the partition function for this \(E\)
One look at the parabola under the height Gaussian is enough. Then jump to the least-squares slide with recognition rather than a new idea.

Quadratic Energy (Regression)

\[ E(\mathbf{w})=\sum_n\left(y_n-\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_n\right)^2 \]

  • Least squares = energy of the residual
  • Gaussian negative log-likelihood (up to additives and scale)
  • Gauss already linked this quadratic energy to the normal density

Cross-Entropy Energy (Classification)

\[ E=-\sum_c y_c\log\hat{y}_c \]

  • Cross-entropy / log-loss scores the predictive distribution
  • Softmax: \(\hat{y}_c\propto e^{-E_c}\) — class scores as energies
  • Same arithmetic as \(\mathbb{E}[-\log p]\) (Shannon entropy of a model)

Boltzmann Machine: Pairwise Energy

\[ E(\mathbf{s})=-\sum_i b_i s_i-\sum_{i<j}W_{ij}s_i s_j \]

  • Binary units \(s_i\in\{0,1\}\) or \(\{\pm 1\}\)
  • Biases plus pairwise couplings — classic BM has no triple terms
  • \(p(\mathbf{s})\propto e^{-E(\mathbf{s})/T}\): a learnable Gibbs distribution
Name only. No contrastive divergence. Hopfield is the fixed-weight cousin: recall as energy descent.

Boltzmann Seed

A Prescription to Interrogate

For fixed mean energy \(U\), the maximum-entropy occupation is the Boltzmann distribution — same \(E\) grammar as the ML energies above.

  • \(p_i \propto e^{-\beta E_i}\) with coldness \(\beta = 1/kT\)
  • Normaliser \(Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}\), so \(p_i = e^{-\beta E_i}/Z\)
  • Week 2: Hydrodynamica / entropy bridge / embodiment; Week 3 clears Gibbs / \(F\) / HMC then Shannon
Do not derive Lagrange multipliers today. State the formula so Worksheet 1 has a concrete claim to open and then press. Students should leave curious about why this exponential, what \(\beta\) means, and whether entropy here is a constraint or a recipe. Point back to cross-entropy / BM pairwise energy if they ask what \(E\) is. Hamiltonian / HMC demos wait for lecture 3. If asked what you gain by calling a loss an energy: the honest answer is that some people hope to avoid \(Z\); LeCun’s energy-based models are that habit.
