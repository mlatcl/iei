From Daniel Bernoulli: Week 1 Carry-Over

Laplace’s Molecule: an Anachronism?

Laplace spoke of “a simple molecule of air.” That sounds anachronistic once we meet Boltzmann.

  • Laplace, Essai (1814): the curve of a molecule is as determined as a planet’s
  • Boltzmann dies in 1906 still fighting atomists’ opponents
  • Atoms / molecules were not settled science in the nineteenth century
Callback to lecture 1 quote. Do not resolve yet, ask why Laplace can talk this way a century before Perrin. Answer is Bernoulli’s kinetic tradition, not modern chemistry.

“Molecule” Before Chemistry Won

Laplace is not citing Dalton. He is speaking Newton’s corpuscular language.

  • French molécule: a small particle of matter, not yet the chemist’s molecule
  • The kinetic idea is older than the chemical proof of atoms
  • The gremlin is ignorance of that particle’s path, not a claim that atoms were established

Hydrodynamica

Daniel Bernoulli (1738) already treats a gas as many tiny bodies in motion.

Wing cartoon: particle lines around an aerofoil that “must” go faster because the path is longer — why do they not just arrive late? You are mixing grains. Keep Johann appropriating the work in notes, not on the slide.

Daniel Bernoulli’s Hydrodynamica published in 1738. It was one of the first works to use the idea of conservation of energy. It used Newton’s laws to predict the behaviour of gases.

Daniel Bernoulli’s chapter on the kinetic theory of gases, for a review on the context of this chapter see Mikhailov (n.d.). For 1738 this is extraordinary thinking. The notion of kinetic theory of gases would not become fully accepted in Physics until 1908 when a model of Einstein’s was verified by Jean Baptiste Perrin.

What Bernoulli Assumes

Hydrodynamica (1738) already treats a gas as many tiny bodies in motion.

  • Many corpuscles, small compared with the vessel
  • Motion is swift and disordered; collisions transfer momentum
  • Pressure on a wall is the rain of impacts, not a static push
  • Idealisation: elastic billiard-like encounters, no lingering forces
Board the assumptions as modelling choices. The billiards canvas makes them literal. Flag what is not assumed: chemical identity of atoms, quantum mechanics, or a finished thermodynamic entropy.

Assumptions Are Already Coarse

Bernoulli does not track Laplace’s curve for every particle — he summarises a swarm.

  • Individual trajectories are discarded
  • What remains is a mechanical story for pressure
  • Next: make the discarded detail a probability — the ensemble

Entropy Billiards

Velocity-bin entropy:
Bernoulli’s simple kinetic models of gases assume that the molecules of air operate like billiard balls. Without small variations in the initial conditions they would never bump into each other. The displayed entropy is the Shannon entropy of the observed discrete velocity histogram (a coarse-grained proxy, not the differential entropy of a Gaussian, and not full thermodynamic entropy).

Maxwell (1831–1879): each Cartesian velocity component of an ideal-gas particle is Gaussian. Speed \(v=|\mathbf{v}|\) is not — it picks up a \(v^2\) Jacobian.

The histogram above is one component \(v_x\). The Maxwell speed distribution is different.

  • Component \(v_x\): Gaussian (what we plotted)
  • Speed \(v=|\mathbf{v}|\): \(f(v)\propto v^2 e^{-mv^2/2kT}\)
  • Week 3: how that change of variables shifts continuous entropy

From Paths to an Ensemble

Laplace’s gremlin says we cannot follow every curve. The ensemble is how we live with that.

  • Microstate: positions and velocities of all particles (Laplace’s curves)
  • Macrostate: a few numbers — \(N\), \(V\), \(E\) or \(T\), \(P\)
  • Ensemble: a probability over the microstates compatible with the macrostate
Name coarse-graining without measure theory. Billiard histogram bins are a toy coarse-graining; \(T,V,P\) are the physicist’s. Do not derive MaxEnt. Die grouping waits for week 3.

Extensive and Intensive

Some macrostate properties scale with the size of the system. Some do not.

  • Extensive: \(N\), \(V\), energy — scale with the system
  • Intensive: temperature — does not
  • Entropy is extensive: \(N\) independent copies \(\Rightarrow\) \(N\) times the entropy
Ask the room: is entropy intensive or extensive? One route: you can calculate one thermodynamic quantity from another, so they must scale roughly the same way. The other: entropy just increases as I increase the state space.

A Hurricane Is Not in the Equations

There is no variable “hurricane” in Navier–Stokes.

  • Just air, water, land — particles moving around
  • We still all agree hurricanes exist
  • Right level of abstraction is how we decide
Niall Robinson, formerly Met Office, now NVIDIA: to find a hurricane in a simulation you write a list of things (wind speed above a threshold, location). Contrast a chair — computer vision struggled for years to write logical rules. Gas/liquid/solid is cleaner. Do not mark students wrong for disagreeing: this is speculation.

The Table Is Not Maximum Entropy

Some of a table’s properties look like macrostates. The rest is structure.

  • Volume and temperature: gas-like summaries
  • Grain, chains in the wood: correlations we are not throwing away
  • The table is not in a maximum-entropy state

Coarse-Graining Is a Choice of Description

Temperature and pressure are sufficient statistics of the swarm

  • Histogram bins: keep some velocity detail, discard the rest
  • Thermodynamics: keep \(T\), \(V\), \(P\); discard which molecule was which
  • The uncertainty we write as entropy lives at the chosen grain

Discretisation Produces Entropy

Finer grain: discard less entropy, compute more.

  • Exact only if you track the continuous thing
  • UK weather: cells of about 800 metres — why not a millimetre?
  • Slow engines are more efficient and less powerful. Is that the same trade-off?
Francisco Vargas, Schrödinger–Föllmer sampler: exact only if the diffusion is run continuously; discretise and you produce entropy. Student may offer higher-order integrators (trapezoid vs Euler): that is mechanism, not the entropic bound — same split as Maxwell’s demon. Do not prove anything. Ask: is the trade-off fundamental, or a coincidence?

Bridge to Maxwell, Boltzmann, Gibbs

They turn Bernoulli’s swarm and Laplace’s ignorance into a calculus.

  • Maxwell: velocity statistics of the ideal gas
  • Boltzmann: \(S\) linked to probability over microstates
  • Gibbs: ensembles as the systematic language

Entropy Review

Computing Expectations Example

  • Consider the following distribution.
\(y\) 1 2 3 4
\(P\left(y\right)\) 0.3 0.2 0.1 0.4
  • What is the mean of the distribution?
  • What is the standard deviation of the distribution?
  • Are the mean and standard deviation representative of the distribution form?
  • What is the expected value of \(-\log P(y)\)?

Expectations Example: Answer

  • We are given:
\(y\) 1 2 3 4
\(P\left(y\right)\) 0.3 0.2 0.1 0.4
\(y^2\) 1 4 9 16
\(-\log(P(y))\) 1.204 1.609 2.302 0.916
  • Mean: \(1\times 0.3 + 2\times 0.2 + 3 \times 0.1 + 4 \times 0.4 = 2.6\)
  • Second moment: \(1 \times 0.3 + 4 \times 0.2 + 9 \times 0.1 + 16 \times 0.4 = 8.4\)
  • Variance: \(8.4 - 2.6\times 2.6 = 1.64\)
  • Standard deviation: \(\sqrt{1.64} = 1.2806\)

Expectations Example: Answer II

  • We are given that:
\(y\) 1 2 3 4
\(P\left(y\right)\) 0.3 0.2 0.1 0.4
\(y^2\) 1 4 9 16
\(-\log(P(y))\) 1.204 1.609 2.302 0.916
  • Expectation \(-\log(P(y))\): \(0.3\times 1.204 + 0.2\times 1.609 + 0.1\times 2.302 +0.4\times 0.916 = 1.280\)

Uncertainty as a Number

Shannon entropy turns a distribution into a scalar. That number must compose across nested coarse-grainings.

  • Discrete: \(H(p) = -\sum_i p_i \log p_i\) — expected surprisal
  • Base 2: bits; natural log: nats
  • Nested grains (this room, then the planet) must add consistently
Fair yes/no questions under optimal play is the operational reading — week 3 with the information game. What you argued today is composition: otherwise information loss at one grain is inconsistent with the grain above.

Entropy of Common Distributions

Entropy, Distribution by Distribution

For each named family from the probability review: plot \(P\), plot \(-\log_2 P\), and form the summands \(-P\log_2 P\).

  • Left: the distribution itself
  • Middle: surprisal \(-\log_2 P\) (not called energy here)
  • Right: \(-P\log_2 P\) — these are what sum (or integrate) to \(H\)
One visual grammar for every family. Keep the middle panel as surprisal'' /\(-\log P\)’‘; reserve ``energy’’ for the ML / Boltzmann block.

Bernoulli and Binomial

  • Bernoulli(\(p\)): binary entropy \(-p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)\)
  • Binomial(\(n,p\)): no elementary closed form; same three-panel sum
  • Fair coin: \(H=1\) bit

Bernoulli Entropy

Bernoulli Entropy

Bernoulli(\(p=0.3\)). Left: the distribution. Middle: surprisal \(-\log_2 P(x)\). Right: the summands \(-P(x)\log_2 P(x)\); their total is the entropy \(H\).

Bernoulli Entropy

  • Fair coin \(p=\tfrac12\): \(H=1\) bit — maximal for a binary variable
  • As \(p\to 0\) or \(1\): \(H\to 0\) — the outcome is nearly certain
  • Closed form: \(H=-p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)\)

Binomial Entropy

Binomial Entropy

Binomial(\(n=12\), \(p=0.4\)). The right-hand panel sums to \(H\); tails contribute little because \(P(k)\) is small even when surprisal is large.

Binomial Entropy

  • Same visual grammar as Bernoulli: \(P\), \(-\log_2 P\), \(-P\log_2 P\)
  • Surprisal is largest in the tails; weight \(P(k)\) suppresses them in \(H\)
  • Large-\(n\) approximation: \(H\approx\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e\, np(1-p))\) bits

Poisson and Multinomial

  • Poisson(\(\lambda\)): numerical sum over \(k\); \(\approx\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e\lambda)\) for large \(\lambda\)
  • Categorical / one multinomial trial: \(H=-\sum_k p_k\log_2 p_k\)
  • Uniform on \(K\) symbols: \(H=\log_2 K\)

Poisson Entropy

Poisson Entropy

Poisson(\(\lambda=4\)). Mid: surprisal rises in the tails. Right: the probability weight cuts those tails back, so \(H\) is dominated by the bulk.

Poisson Entropy

  • Count distributions: same three-panel reading as Bernoulli / binomial
  • Large-\(\lambda\) approximation: \(H\approx\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e\lambda)\) bits
  • Entropy grows only logarithmically with the rate

Multinomial / Categorical Entropy

Categorical Entropy

The four-outcome distribution from the expectation example, now in bits. Right-hand summands total \(H\); category \(y=3\) is rarest (highest surprisal) but contributes modestly because \(P(3)\) is small.

Categorical Entropy

  • One multinomial trial \(=\) categorical: \(H=-\sum_k p_k\log_2 p_k\)
  • Uniform on \(K\) symbols: \(H=\log_2 K\) — maximal
  • \(n\) i.i.d. trials: entropies add; the count vector is more subtle

Gaussian

  • Differential entropy \(h=-\int p\log_2 p\,\mathrm{d}y\)
  • Univariate Gaussian: \(h=\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e\sigma^2)\) bits
  • Can be negative; not the same object as discrete \(H\)

Gaussian Differential Entropy

Gaussian Differential Entropy

Univariate Gaussian (height example). Middle: \(-\log_2 p(y)\); dips below zero where the density exceeds \(1\). Right: the integrand whose integral is differential entropy \(h\).

Gaussian Differential Entropy

  • Closed form: \(h=\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e\sigma^2)\) bits
  • Grows with \(\sigma\); independent of \(\mu\)
  • Differential entropy \(\neq\) discrete Shannon \(H\) — can be negative
Flag the continuous caveat once. Do not dwell; the three-panel analogy is the point. Discrete \(H\ge 0\); differential \(h\) is a relative measure.

Entropy: physical and anthropomorphic

  • Thermodynamic \(S\) and Shannon \(H\): same form; in equilibrium \(S = k_B H\)
  • \(k_B\) gives physical units (J/K); \(TS\) has units of energy
  • Which degrees of freedom you count is a choice (Jaynes / Wigner: “anthropomorphic”)
  • Steam tables: untampered extras (e.g. electric field) or apparent second-law violations

Joint, Conditional, Chain Rule

  • \(H(X,Y)\) joint uncertainty
  • \(H(X\mid Y)\) residual uncertainty after observing \(Y\)
  • Chain rule: \(H(X,Y)=H(Y)+H(X\mid Y)\)
  • Grouping: \(H(X)=H(G)+H(X\mid G)\) when \(G\) is a function of \(X\)

Emulate, Do Not Replicate

Anything we can measure, a machine can probably emulate. That is not the same as replicating us.

  • Our intelligence is shaped by its context
  • Measure a capability, fit parameters, recreate the score
  • You cannot simply replicate us, because the context is specific
The cover is not the argument. One accidental equation in the book (\(E=mc^2\)) because everyone knows it. Do not define intelligence. Ask whether AGI-as-better-on-all-scores is even the right question.

The Cut

If every machine capability slices something away from us, does the slicing go forever?

  • Greek atomists: you stop — the world is made of what you cannot cut
  • Computer scientists often say no: humans disappear
  • Other answers: empathy, suffering, God
Democritus, not a physics lecture. Accelerators splitting the atom just means we misnamed the atom: quarks sit below. In a public-understanding talk, pause here.

What the Machine Cannot Have

The machine cannot have our weaknesses and our vulnerabilities. They are intrinsic to us.

  • Anything I can score, I can emulate
  • Vulnerabilities and limitations are fundamental
  • Key among them: communicate versus compute

Culture Is Compression

We built a cultural landscape so that a few bits can point at an idea you already know.

  • Analogies remain a puzzle — why they work is not a derived principle
  • AGI without that limitation is not general. It is specific to us
  • You can emulate it, but it is not the same
The argument is Shannon’s bits: set aside conversation about intelligence and ask how much information is coming in over what time. The machine is already six orders of magnitude better off by the physics.
