Liouville, Detailed Balance, and the Trapdoor

Three Levels of the Same Objection

Same impossibility; different grain.

  • Liouville — phase-space volume under reversible flow
  • Detailed balance — equilibrium rates of micro-transitions
  • Course fork — two ledgers under Maxwellians-to-gate
Open the teach-back here. Students met all three in dialogues; today map them. Do not board Hamilton’s equations.

Liouville (What We Need)

Reversible microscopic dynamics cannot throw volume away.

  • Track a cloud of microstates in phase space \((\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})\)
  • Hamiltonian flow deforms the cloud; its volume stays constant
  • Fine-grained Shannon entropy of the density is conserved
Picture only: a blob that stretches and folds but keeps its area. No symplectic form. Link to Ehrenfest: only coarse-grained entropy can grow.

Detailed Balance (What We Need)

At equilibrium, each transition is cancelled by its reverse.

  • Microscopic reversibility: laws look the same under time reversal
  • Stationarity alone is weaker: net currents among groups can still vanish
  • Detailed balance: rate \(A\to B\) equals rate \(B\to A\) for each pair
Distinguish flux balance (stationarity of a ledger) from detailed balance (each reverse pair). Course terminology from Week 4: membrane number-flux stationarity is flux balance, not detailed balance.

How the Course Fork Fits

  • Illegal grain: every gate velocity drawn from a bulk Maxwellian
  • Number flux can balance; energy flux then cannot (or conversely)
  • Same moral: you cannot have a one-way sorter at equilibrium for free
One sentence back to lecture 4. Do not re-derive \(\Phi^{\mathrm{hot}}=\Phi^{\mathrm{cold}}\). The point is the map: fork \(\leftrightarrow\) detailed balance \(\leftrightarrow\) Liouville.

###{Good Regulator Overview}

Good Regulator Overview

Notation: \(D\), \(S\), \(A\), \(Z\)

The CGF was \(\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\). After the break, \(A\) is action.

  • \(D\): disturbance
  • \(S\): system state
  • \(A\): action (the regulator’s response; Conant–Ashby often write \(R\))
  • \(Z\): reguland / outcome (Ashby’s essential variable)
Board the four letters once. Classic cybernetics used \(R\) for the regulator; we use \(A\) for action so the policy language matches Weeks 7–8. The log-partition / CGF is \(\psi\), so there is no clash with action. Do not prove anything yet.

Two Setups

Cause-controlled regulation (left) acts on the disturbance \(D\); error-controlled regulation (right) acts on the state \(S\) that \(D\) caused.
  • Cause-controlled: act on \(D\) (or on a model of \(D\))
  • Error-controlled: act on the state \(S\) that \(D\) caused
  • DPI: \(I(A;D)\le I(S;D)\) when \(A\) only sees \(S\)
Spend the clock on the diagrams. Cause control reads the disturbance before it enters the essential variable; error control corrects after the fact. Most modern RL policies are error-controlled on the observed state. Week 8 returns to why the simplest optimum factors as a model.

What ``Good’’ Means Today

Success is low entropy in the reguland — with two caveats.

  • Objective sketch: \(\min_{\pi(a\mid s)} H(Z)\)
  • Slogan: every good regulator must be a model of the system
  • Weak reading of ``model’’: \(H(A\mid S)=0\), i.e. \(A=h(S)\)
  • Full theorem and caveats: Week 8 review
Name the objective and the slogan. Do not board the concavity transfer \(g(\alpha)\). Two fences only: constant \(A=a_0\) also has \(H(A\mid S)=0\); low \(H(Z)\) is predictable, not desirable. Worksheet 4 Exchange 1 has already asked them what GRT has to do with general intelligence — leave the depth for Week 8.

Shannon Capacity and DPI

Shannon’s Noisy Channel Coding Theorem

A channel is a conditional \(p(y\mid x)\). Rate cannot beat capacity.

  • Mutual information \(I(X;Y)\) is the channel currency
  • Capacity \(C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)\)
  • No-go: reliable communication requires \(R \le C\)
  • Prescription: the input \(p(x)\) that achieves \(C\)
LO10. Carnot twin: forbids rates above \(C\); does not construct a code. BSC plot from the capacity include if time.

Scaffolding, Not Outcomes

Chain Rule, Mutual Information, Capacity

Four results we will need later — stated, not proved today.

  • Chain rule: \(H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y|X)\)
  • Mutual information: \(I(X;Y) = H(X)-H(X|Y) = H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)\)
  • Capacity: no rate above \(C\); achieving \(C\) requires the capacity-achieving \(p(x)\)
  • Data processing: if \(X\to Y\to Z\) is Markov, \(I(X;Z)\le I(X;Y)\)
Define \(I\) from the chain rule. State DPI; do not prove. Proof and the information bottleneck are week 8 (LO10). Capacity remains this week’s no-go/prescription pair.
Mutual information for a binary symmetric channel; capacity is achieved at uniform input.

Data-Processing Inequality

If \(X\to Y\to Z\) is Markov, processing cannot create information about \(X\).

  • Markov: \(p(z\mid x,y)=p(z\mid y)\)
  • Statement: \(I(X;Z)\le I(X;Y)\)
  • Soft partitions (Week 5): leftover \(H(G\mid X)\) already spent information
  • Full proof: notes / Week 8 enrichment — not today’s board
LO10 moral only. You cannot process your way to more \(I\). Underwrites requisite variety and GRT tools.

Conceptually, imagine a classic physics setup where you can see inside a box: there are a number of particles moving around, sampled from random Gaussians. If you plot their velocities, they sit within a circle. In this case, the marginal \(h_i\) would be their velocity in each direction. The marginal distribution in each direction is Gaussian. Now, if the particles are correlated — everything tending to move in one diagonal direction — interestingly, the marginal entropies are the same. Although these are correlated, I’m not saying anything about the correlation when I conserve the marginal entropies, because if you look at the marginal distribution in each direction, it’s the same as in the uncorrelated case. And if they’re anti-correlated, it’s the same again. So I’m not making any statements about independence with this constraint.

Optional overflow: DPI Proof and Information Bottleneck

Data-Processing Inequality

Data-Processing Inequality

If \(X\to Y\to Z\) is a Markov chain, processing cannot create information about \(X\).

  • Markov: \(p(z\mid x,y)=p(z\mid y)\), equivalently \(I(X;Z\mid Y)=0\)
  • \(I(X;Y,Z)=I(X;Y)+I(X;Z\mid Y)=I(X;Y)\)
  • \(I(X;Y,Z)=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y\mid Z)\ge I(X;Z)\)
  • Therefore \(I(X;Z)\le I(X;Y)\)
LO10. Cover and Thomas Theorem 2.8.1. Board the two expansions of \(I(X;Y,Z)\). No-go: you cannot process your way to more \(I\).

KL Decay and Grouping

Under Markov dynamics, \(\text{KL}\left( p_t\,\|\,\pi \right)\) falls — coarse-graining in time.

  • Physics often says ``relative entropy’’; same object as week-3 / week-6 KL
  • Data-processing for distributions: each step cannot increase divergence to equilibrium
  • Same grouping split as the die, now for approach to \(\pi\):

\[ \text{KL}\left( p\,\|\,\pi \right)=\text{KL}\left( w\,\|\,w_\pi \right)+\sum_i w_i\, \text{KL}\left( p_i\,\|\,\pi_i \right) \]

After proving DPI for mutual information, give the dynamical face. Course notation is \(\text{KL}\left( \cdot\,\|\,\cdot \right)\) throughout; ``relative entropy’’ is optional physics prose. Board the split: between-group redistribution versus within-group relaxation. Hamiltonian aside: fine-grained \(H\) conserved; only coarse-grained entropy grows (Ehrenfest).

Arrow of Time from the Same Split

How much of equilibration is between groups, how much within?

  • Between: \(\text{KL}\left( w\,\|\,w_\pi \right)\) — redistribution of macro probability
  • Within: \(\sum_i w_i \text{KL}\left( p_i\,\|\,\pi_i \right)\) — relaxation to local \(\pi_i\)
  • Demon / Crooks / transport: all move mass among groups while baths handle the rest

Information Bottleneck

Information Bottleneck

Least-committal \(T\) given a relevance constraint — MaxEnt, now on mutual information.

  • Markov: \(Y \leftrightarrow X \leftrightarrow T\)
  • Minimise \(I(X;T)\) subject to \(I(T;Y)\ge R\)
  • Lagrangian: \(I(X;T)-\beta I(T;Y)\)
  • DPI bound: \(I(T;Y)\le I(X;Y)\)
LO10. \(\beta\to 0\) discards everything; \(\beta\to\infty\) keeps \(T=X\). Optimal \(p(t\mid x)\) is Boltzmann / exponential family. Week 8 uses this as a tool, not a new outcome.
